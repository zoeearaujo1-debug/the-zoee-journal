The world, in perspective

Ecuador · Environment

El Niño: the numbers behind Ecuador's rainy season

By November 23, Ecuador's National Secretariat for Risk Management had recorded 1,883 rain-related adverse events since August 29. The events affected 24 provinces, 173 cantons and 580 parishes, with 110,855 people impacted. Flooding accounted for nearly three in five reported events. The figures make this more than a weather story: they point to pressure on homes, roads, local services and livelihoods.

What it means

Climate risk is also economic risk

When floods interrupt transport, damage homes or affect agricultural areas, the consequences move through the economy. Businesses face delays, families absorb repair costs, and public budgets must fund response and reconstruction. Thinking about climate resilience therefore belongs in conversations about productivity, insurance, infrastructure and public finance — not only environmental policy.

The Journal's perspective: the most useful question is not simply how much rain falls, but how prepared communities are for what follows.

Work, data & ideas

Data & decision-making

A dashboard is not the answer. It's the beginning.

A dashboard can show what happened. Analysis begins when someone asks why it happened, what might happen next and which decision the evidence supports. A useful analyst makes assumptions visible, checks whether a metric actually represents the thing it claims to measure, and explains uncertainty in plain language. The deliverable is not the chart; it is a better-informed decision.

Leadership

Clarity is a form of respect

People do better work when they understand the outcome, the reason behind it and the boundaries they can make decisions within. A practical leadership habit: when delegating, explain the purpose, define what “done” looks like, agree on a check-in point and leave room for the person to choose the route. Autonomy works best when expectations are clear.

Higher education

The degree is a foundation, not the finish line

As work changes, the value of higher education increasingly depends on how well it connects concepts with practice. Projects, research literacy, communication and the ability to keep learning matter alongside formal credentials. For students and institutions alike, the question is how to build knowledge that remains useful after the course ends.

The style file

Build a point of view, not a costume

Trends are most useful as a source of ideas. Notice the proportions, textures and colours you keep returning to, then adapt one new element at a time. A wardrobe with a clear point of view often feels more current than one built by chasing every micro-trend.
